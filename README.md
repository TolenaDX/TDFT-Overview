
# TDFT — Tolena Digital Financial Technology

### Blockchain-Native Infrastructure for Programmable Global Trade Finance

**Developed by Tolena Digital Technology**

TDFT is a blockchain-native financial technology infrastructure designed to modernize global trade finance through programmable and conditional financial instruments, independent verification, controlled settlement, and rule-based contractual execution.

The platform combines blockchain technology with structured financial workflows to support businesses, banks, financial institutions, and international trade participants.

TDFT is designed around a comprehensive portfolio of **ten digital financial instruments**, enabling financial obligations and commercial transactions to be managed through defined contractual conditions, authorized participants, and transparent execution processes.

Its objective is to improve the efficiency, transparency, predictability, and operational control of global financial transactions.

## 2. Ten Programmable Financial Instruments

TDFT's overall architecture is designed to support **ten programmable financial instruments** covering trade finance, financial guarantees, contractual payments, and related institutional financial operations.

The initial implementation focuses on three advanced instruments: **DCC, DGI, and DSC**.

DCC has successfully completed internal end-to-end validation, including transaction workflows and settlement execution. DGI and DSC have reached an advanced stage of technical development and internal validation.

The remaining seven instruments form part of TDFT's broader development roadmap.

### Initial Three Financial Instruments

#### 1. DCC — Digital Credit Contract

The Digital Credit Contract is a programmable trade-finance instrument designed to provide an alternative operational workflow to traditional documentary letters of credit.

DCC supports:

- Digital creation and acceptance of contractual obligations
- Defined buyer and beneficiary responsibilities
- Conditional allocation and control of settlement funds
- Independent verification of contractual performance
- Rule-based settlement authorization
- Contractually defined adjustment and refund outcomes
- Milestone-based execution where applicable
- Transparent transaction status and execution records

DCC is the first instrument prioritized for institutional commercial introduction.

#### 2. DGI — Digital Guarantee Instrument

The Digital Guarantee Instrument provides a programmable framework for managing conditional financial guarantee obligations.

It is designed to support defined guarantee conditions, authorized participants, controlled execution, and transparent transaction records.

DGI is intended to provide a digital operational framework for financial guarantees within institutional and commercial environments.

#### 3. DSC — Digital Standby Credit

The Digital Standby Credit is a programmable standby financial instrument designed around predefined obligations, conditional claims, and controlled execution procedures.

DSC is intended to support standby financial commitments through structured digital workflows and authorized contractual processes.

### Seven Additional Financial Instruments

The broader TDFT development roadmap includes seven additional financial instruments.

These instruments are intended to expand the platform's capabilities across trade finance, contractual obligations, financing arrangements, and institutional financial operations.

Their implementation, validation, and commercial introduction will follow the platform's development priorities and institutional requirements.

The ten-instrument portfolio represents TDFT's broader infrastructure strategy. The instruments are not all at the same stage of development or deployment readiness.

## 3. Conditional Settlement

Conditional settlement is a central principle of TDFT.

Instead of relying exclusively on manual payment instructions, TDFT enables settlement actions to be governed by predefined contractual conditions and authorized verification processes.

A typical DCC transaction may involve:

1. Creation of the digital financial instrument.
2. Review and acceptance by the designated parties.
3. Allocation of the required settlement funds.
4. Activation of the contractual workflow.
5. Performance of the underlying commercial obligations.
6. Independent verification of the agreed conditions.
7. Execution of the contractually permitted settlement outcome.

Depending on the contractual conditions, permitted outcomes may include:

- Payment to the beneficiary following verified contractual performance.
- A settlement adjustment agreed by the authorized contractual parties.
- Return of settlement funds to the buyer when the applicable contractual conditions require it.

The objective is to provide predictable execution while preserving defined contractual rights, responsibilities, and settlement safeguards.

## 4. Independent Verification and Participant Roles

TDFT separates contractual participation, independent verification, and settlement execution responsibilities.

For DCC, the operating model includes designated roles such as:

- **Issuer:** Initiates and structures the digital financial instrument.
- **Buyer / Funding Party:** Provides the designated settlement funds.
- **Seller / Beneficiary:** Receives settlement when the contractual conditions are satisfied.
- **Inspector / Verifier:** Independently assesses the relevant contractual performance conditions.
- **Settlement Authority:** Executes permitted settlement actions according to the contractual workflow.

This separation is designed to strengthen operational accountability, reduce reliance on unilateral payment decisions, and support transparent transaction management.

The Inspector verifies contractual performance but does not independently control the settlement funds.

Settlement execution follows the authorized contractual workflow and its predefined conditions.

## 5. Programmable Contractual Execution

TDFT is designed to translate agreed financial obligations into structured digital workflows.

Depending on the financial instrument, programmable conditions may govern:

- Contractual acceptance and activation
- Participant authorization
- Funding requirements

- Milestone completion

The objective is to reduce unnecessary manual intervention while maintaining clear participant responsibilities and controlled execution.

Programmability does not eliminate the need for legal agreements, compliance procedures, or institutional oversight.
